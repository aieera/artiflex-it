## What modern endpoint security actually has to do

Every device that connects to your network — laptop, desktop, server, mobile, virtual machine — is an attack vector. That has been true since 1985, but what an "endpoint security product" is expected to do in 2026 is unrecognisable from the antivirus of even ten years ago.

For UAE businesses, modern endpoint protection has to do six concrete jobs at the same time. Detect threats *without* relying on signatures, because zero-days and fileless malware never appear in any signature database. Record full endpoint telemetry — every process, file, registry change, network connection — so investigators can reconstruct what happened, not just be told that something did. Roll back ransomware automatically, restoring encrypted files to their clean state in real time. Correlate endpoint events with email, identity, network, and cloud telemetry so the unified attack narrative is visible across the kill chain, not stuck in five different consoles. Deliver evidence — logs, audit trails, MTTR/MTTD metrics — that satisfies a NESA auditor or UAE PDPL controller. And do all of that on a single lightweight agent that does not destroy the device's performance.

If your current endpoint product is doing fewer than four of those six jobs, you are running a 2015 stack against a 2026 threat landscape.

## How endpoint protection has evolved

Endpoint security is on its fourth major architectural generation. The right platform for your business depends on which generation you are running today and where the threats your business actually faces sit.

### Antivirus (Generation 1, 1989)

Signature-based detection — file content matched against a database of known malware. Effective against threats already catalogued by analysts, blind to anything new. Still useful as a first-pass filter; insufficient on its own as a primary defence.

### Next-Generation AV (Generation 2, 2012–2017)

Behavioural analysis, machine learning, and memory scanning replaced pure signature matching. Detection logic moved from what the file *is* to what it *does* — catching ransomware, fileless malware, and zero-days that signature scanners cannot see. This is the floor for modern endpoint protection.

### EDR — Endpoint Detection and Response (Generation 3, 2017–present)

NGAV plus full endpoint telemetry recording, threat hunting, automated response (isolate, quarantine, rollback), and forensic investigation. Security teams gain complete visibility into what happened on every endpoint before, during, and after an attack. The current minimum for any organisation with a security team capable of operating it.

### XDR — Extended Detection and Response (Generation 4, 2020–present)

EDR's endpoint visibility, correlated with network, email, cloud workload, and identity telemetry, into a unified attack narrative across the entire kill chain. Eliminates the blind spots between siloed tools. The right answer for any UAE enterprise running hybrid infrastructure or SaaS-heavy stacks.

### MDR — Managed Detection and Response

Not a generation but a delivery model. Fully managed 24/7 detection and response, run by an external SOC against your endpoint data. The right answer for organisations that need EDR/XDR-grade coverage without building or paying for an in-house security operations team.

The big 2025 development: following the USD 859M acquisition of Secureworks in February 2025, Intercept X endpoint telemetry feeds directly into the Taegis SIEM/XDR platform — giving organisations enterprise-grade SIEM correlation, 20+ years of attack detection data, and the Counter Threat Unit's threat intelligence applied to endpoint events. Sophos MDR powered by Secureworks is now the world's largest MDR service, protecting 28,000+ organisations.

Single lightweight agent, managed entirely from Sophos Central — covers Windows, macOS, Linux, servers, and mobile under one licence. TCO is typically 35–40% lower than CrowdStrike Falcon for equivalent capabilities.

CrowdStrike pioneered the cloud-native EDR approach with Falcon — a single lightweight agent streaming telemetry to the cloud for AI-powered detection and response. The Falcon platform covers endpoint protection, threat intelligence, identity protection, and cloud workload security. Falcon X delivers automated threat intelligence — investigates every detection and produces a finished threat-intelligence report within minutes, eliminating manual analyst enrichment. ### Microsoft Defender for Endpoint — Best Value for M365 E5 Customers

Microsoft Defender for Endpoint (MDE) is deeply integrated into Windows — providing behaviour monitoring, attack surface reduction rules, and EDR capabilities built into the operating system itself. No separate agent installation is required on Windows 10/11 or Windows Server. For organisations licensed on Microsoft 365 E5 or Microsoft Defender for Business, MDE is included at no additional cost — making it the best value endpoint solution for Microsoft-centric organisations already on E5. Microsoft Copilot for Security provides AI-assisted investigation: analysts query incidents in natural language and receive automated attack-chain summaries and recommended responses. Deep integration with Microsoft Sentinel (SIEM), Entra ID (identity), Purview (DLP), and Intune (device management) — providing a unified Microsoft security stack.

**Limitation:** effectiveness is most pronounced within the Microsoft ecosystem. Mixed environments with Linux, macOS, or non-Microsoft cloud may find coverage gaps compared to dedicated cross-platform EDR vendors.

**Best fit:** UAE organisations standardised on Microsoft 365 E5 — particularly banking, public sector, and education environments where the Microsoft stack is already the production environment.

SentinelOne is the only major EDR platform to offer fully autonomous threat response — the AI agent can detect, contain, and remediate threats entirely without human intervention, even when offline (no cloud connectivity required). Storyline technology automatically stitches together all related events into a causal narrative — showing analysts exactly how an attack unfolded from initial access to impact, dramatically reducing investigation time. Purple AI is a generative AI security analyst built into the platform — security teams query their entire environment in natural language to hunt threats, investigate incidents, and generate reports. Singularity Data Lake ingests data from any source, not just SentinelOne agents — providing SIEM-like capabilities alongside endpoint protection. Strong anti-ransomware with automatic rollback. Vigilance MDR also available.

**Pricing:** mid-range — more expensive than Sophos but less than CrowdStrike for comparable features.

**Best fit:** UAE organisations with disconnected endpoints (field operations, OT-adjacent environments) where offline autonomous response matters, and security teams who want generative-AI-driven hunting without a separate SIEM.

Available as cloud-hosted SaaS or on-premises deployment — giving organisations in heavily regulated industries with data sovereignty requirements the option to keep their security console within their own infrastructure.

Deep experience in protecting OT and ICS environments alongside IT endpoints — making Vision One a strong choice for manufacturing, energy, and critical infrastructure organisations.

Check Point Harmony Endpoint delivers the same proven threat-prevention technology used in Check Point's network security gateways, directly on the endpoint. ## How we choose the right endpoint platform — the four-question matrix

Procurement decisions get cleaner when the questions are direct.

1. **What is your existing security stack?** Microsoft 365 E5 with Sentinel and Entra → Defender for Endpoint is usually the best-value answer. Sophos Firewall + Sophos Email → Intercept X with Synchronized Security wins on automation. Check Point Quantum estate → Harmony Endpoint reduces operational friction. None of the above → Sophos vs. CrowdStrike vs. SentinelOne becomes the conversation, judged on TCO and ops capacity.
2. **What is your operational capacity?** A small in-house IT team needs single-pane management (Sophos Central, Microsoft Defender) and an MDR option. A mature security-engineering team can run CrowdStrike Falcon or SentinelOne Singularity at depth. Match the platform to the operators, not the brochure.
3. **What is your threat model?** Manufacturing, OT, and ICS environments → Trend Micro Vision One. Regulated industries with on-prem console requirements → Bitdefender GravityZone. Hybrid offices with field-deployed endpoints frequently offline → SentinelOne (offline autonomous response). Mainstream UAE enterprise → Sophos Intercept X.
4. **Do you need MDR?** If your team cannot operate EDR 24/7 — and most UAE mid-market teams cannot — MDR is the right answer regardless of the underlying agent. Sophos MDR (now backed by Secureworks Taegis), CrowdStrike Falcon Complete (with breach-prevention warranty), or a partner-delivered MDR — the choice depends on budget envelope and the SLAs you actually need.
