## The NIST Cybersecurity Framework: Your Foundation

Before diving into the roadmap, you need a framework. I recommend the NIST Cybersecurity Framework because it's comprehensive, flexible, and universally recognised. NIST cybersecurity framework implementation organises security into five core functions:

- **Identify:** Know your assets, data, users, and risks. You can't protect what you don't know about.
- **Protect:** Deploy preventive controls: firewalls, endpoint security, access management, encryption.
- **Detect:** Implement monitoring and detection: SIEM, EDR alerts, anomaly detection.
- **Respond:** Have incident response plans and teams ready. Practice them regularly.
- **Recover:** Business continuity, disaster recovery, backup verification, lessons learned.

## The 30-60-90 Day Cybersecurity Plan

For organisations just starting or resetting their security programme, here's the 30 60 90 day cybersecurity plan I've used successfully across multiple engagements:

### Days 1–30: Foundation & Quick Wins

This phase is about visibility and plugging the most critical gaps:

- Conduct a cybersecurity risk assessment — identify your crown jewel data and biggest exposure points
- Deploy MFA across all critical systems (email, VPN, admin consoles)
- Ensure endpoint protection is installed and active on every device
- Review and harden firewall rules — remove any-any rules, disable unused ports
- Implement email security basics: enable DMARC, DKIM, SPF
- Document your incident response plan (even a basic one-pager is better than nothing)

### Days 31–60: Hardening & Detection

Now you build on the foundation with deeper controls:

- Deploy or upgrade to EDR across all endpoints
- Implement DLP policies for your most sensitive data categories
- Set up centralized log collection (SIEM or cloud-native logging)
- Conduct a vulnerability scan and prioritize remediation of critical findings
- Establish a cybersecurity policy covering acceptable use, password requirements, and data handling
- Begin security awareness training for all employees

### Days 61–90: Maturity & Visibility

Phase three builds operational maturity:

- Deploy SIEM or engage MDR for 24/7 monitoring
- Implement zero trust / SASE for remote access
- Conduct your first penetration test
- Establish metrics: mean-time-to-detect, mean-time-to-respond, patch compliance rate
- Present findings and a 12-month roadmap to leadership with budget requirements
- Schedule quarterly security reviews

## Cybersecurity Budget Planning: What to Expect

The perennial question: how much should we spend? The standard guidance is 10–15% of IT budget on security, but that number is nearly meaningless without context. Here's a more practical framework for cybersecurity budget planning:

| Company Size              | Typical Annual Spend | Where the Money Goes                                         |
|---------------------------|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| SMB (50–200 employees)    | USD 50K–150K         | EDR, firewall, email security, basic SIEM, annual pen test   |
| Mid-Market (200–1,000)    | USD 150K–500K        | Above + MDR service, DLP, SASE, quarterly pen tests          |
| Enterprise (1,000–5,000)  | USD 500K–2M          | Full stack + dedicated SOC or managed SOC, SOAR, red teaming |
| Large Enterprise (5,000+) | USD 2M+              | Above + custom integrations, threat intel, 24/7 internal SOC |

## Measuring Your Cybersecurity Maturity

A cybersecurity maturity model helps you benchmark where you are and where you need to be. I use a simplified 5-level model:

**Level 1 — Ad Hoc:** No formal security programme. Reactive only. Most small businesses start here.

**Level 2 — Developing:** Basic controls in place (firewall, antivirus). Some policies documented. No monitoring.

**Level 3 — Defined:** Formal cybersecurity plan, EDR, email security, DLP. Basic monitoring. Regular assessments.

**Level 4 — Managed:** Full security stack, SIEM/MDR, zero trust. Metrics-driven. Regular pen tests and red teaming.

**Level 5 — Optimised:** Proactive threat hunting, AI-driven detection, continuous improvement. Security enables business.

Most organisations I work with are at Level 2–3. The goal isn't to reach Level 5 overnight — it's to advance one level per year through structured investment and how to build a cybersecurity program that's sustainable.

## Cybersecurity ROI: Making the Business Case

Convincing leadership to invest in security requires speaking their language. Cybersecurity ROI for business isn't about theoretical threat reduction — it's about quantifiable risk reduction:

- **Cost avoidance:** Average breach cost (USD 4.88M) multiplied by your probability of breach (varies by industry, but IBM reports 1 in 3 companies experience a breach over 2 years)
- **Insurance savings:** Companies with mature security programmes pay 20–40% less for cyber insurance premiums
- **Revenue enablement:** Security certifications (SOC 2, ISO 27001) unlock enterprise customers who require them
- **Compliance savings:** Proactive security costs a fraction of reactive regulatory fines

Frame every security investment in these terms and you'll find leadership much more receptive to cybersecurity budget planning conversations.
