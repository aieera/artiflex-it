## Why Traditional Email Filters Aren't Enough

If you're relying solely on Microsoft 365's built-in protection or Google Workspace's spam filter, you're leaving a massive gap. These tools are decent at catching commodity spam and known phishing templates, but they struggle with targeted attacks. Business email compromise (BEC) — where an attacker impersonates a CEO or vendor — uses no malware and no malicious links. It's pure social engineering, and it bypasses traditional email security gateways completely.

BEC cost organisations USD 2.9 billion in 2023 alone. These aren't sophisticated technical attacks. They're carefully crafted emails that exploit human trust. A CFO gets an email that appears to come from the CEO: "Please wire USD 350K to this vendor — it's urgent and confidential." No malicious attachment. No suspicious link. Just a well-crafted lie.

## AI Phishing Detection: The New Standard

This is where AI phishing detection is changing the game. Instead of looking for known threat signatures, AI-powered email security analyses communication patterns. It learns how your CEO actually writes, what time they typically send emails, which vendors they interact with. When something deviates from that pattern — say, the "CEO" sends an urgent wire request at 2am from an unfamiliar reply-to address — the system flags it immediately.

Platforms like Abnormal Security, Proofpoint, and Mimecast have built sophisticated behavioural models that catch BEC attempts with remarkable accuracy. In my testing, AI-powered phishing protection for enterprise catches 30–40% more threats than traditional gateway-only approaches.

## How to Prevent Phishing Attacks: A Multi-Layer Approach

Effective phishing prevention solutions combine technology with human awareness. Here's the stack I recommend:

- **Layer 1 — Secure Email Gateway:** Pre-delivery scanning catches bulk phishing, malware attachments, and known-bad URLs. This stops 85–90% of email threats.
- **Layer 2 — AI/ML Post-Delivery Protection:** Analyses emails that pass the gateway. Catches BEC, social engineering, and zero-day threats using behavioural analysis.
- **Layer 3 — DMARC/DKIM/SPF:** DMARC email security prevents attackers from spoofing your domain. If you haven't set up DMARC, you're letting criminals send emails as your company.
- **Layer 4 — Security Awareness Training:** Regular phishing simulations and training. The goal isn't to shame employees — it's to build muscle memory so they spot red flags automatically.
- **Layer 5 — Incident Response Playbook:** When (not if) someone clicks, have a documented process: report button, automatic quarantine, credential reset, and forensic investigation.

## Email Security Best Practices for 2026

Beyond the technology, here are the email security best practices I drill into every client:

- Enforce MFA on all email accounts — credential theft is the most common entry point.
- Implement DMARC at enforcement level (p=reject), not just monitoring (p=none). Half-measures don't work.
- Disable auto-forwarding rules to external addresses — attackers use these to silently exfiltrate data.
- Review mail flow rules quarterly — rogue rules are a classic persistence mechanism.
- Train employees monthly, not annually. Annual training is a compliance checkbox, not actual security.
